IRM2: A Neural Short-Rate Model for Joint Calibration of Treasury Yield Curves and Futures

Mathematical specification, diagnostics, and open problems

Project report · June 2026 · companion to report/project_report.tex

Abstract. IRM2 calibrates a latent-state neural stochastic differential equation (SDE) to the U.S. Treasury yield curve and Treasury futures. A recurrent encoder maps a window of observed curves to a latent initial condition; a Neural SDE evolves it under the risk-neutral measure $\QQ$; a decoder produces the short rate; and a Monte-Carlo pricer values zero-coupon bonds and the cheapest-to-deliver futures option. This document gives the precise formulation, the sequence of identifiability problems uncovered during development (volatility, the term premium, and the decoupling of the futures head), the terms added to resolve them, and the remaining open questions.

1. State-space construction

Let $\mathbf c_t\in\R^M$ be the observed yield curve at date $t$ ($M$ constant-maturity SVENY pillars) and $x_t\in\R$ the observed overnight short rate. An encoder $E_\phi$ (a bidirectional LSTM) compresses a lookback window into a latent initial state

$$ z_0 = E_\phi\!\big(\mathbf c_{t-L:t},\,x_{t-L:t}\big)\in\R^d . $$

The latent state evolves under a Neural SDE with learnable drift $\mu_\theta$ and diagonal diffusion $\sigma_\theta$,

$$ \dd z_s = \mu_\theta(s,z_s)\,\dd s + \sigma_\theta(s,z_s)\,\dd W_s^{\QQ}, \qquad s\in[0,T_{\max}], $$

integrated by Euler–Maruyama with step $\Delta=1/\texttt{spy}$. Two drift families are supported,

$$ \text{(simple)}\ \ \mu_\theta=f_\theta(s,z), \qquad \text{(OU)}\ \ \mu_\theta=\kappa_\theta(s,z)\big(\vartheta_\theta(s,z)-z\big), \quad 0\le\kappa_\theta\le\kappa_{\max}. $$

A decoder $D_\psi:\R^d\to\R$ produces the short rate with an additive anchor pinning the front to the observed overnight rate,

$$ r_s = D_\psi(z_s) + \big(x_t - D_\psi(z_0)\big) \;\Longrightarrow\; r_0 = x_t . $$

All Monte-Carlo paths share $z_0$, so the bracket is deterministic and $r_s = x_t + \big(D_\psi(z_s)-D_\psi(z_0)\big)$: the short-rate dynamics are the decoder's response to the latent displacement, anchored at $x_t$.

2. Risk-neutral pricing

Zero-coupon bonds and yields

$$ P(0,\tau)=\E^{\QQ}\!\Big[e^{-\int_0^\tau r_s\,\dd s}\Big], \qquad y(\tau)=-\frac{\log P(0,\tau)}{\tau}, $$

computed in log-space for stability, $\log P(0,\tau)=\operatorname*{logsumexp}_{n}(-I^{(n)}_\tau)-\log N$ with $I^{(n)}_\tau=\Delta\sum_{s\le\tau} r^{(n)}_s$.

Convexity decomposition

A second-order cumulant expansion of $\log\E^{\QQ}[e^{-J}]$, $J=\int_0^\tau D_\psi(z_s)\dd s$, gives

$$ y(\tau)\approx \underbrace{(x_t-D_0)}_{\text{level}} +\underbrace{\tfrac1\tau\!\int_0^\tau\!\E[D_\psi(z_s)]\dd s}_{\text{expectations}} -\underbrace{\tfrac1{2\tau}\Var\!\Big(\!\int_0^\tau\! D_\psi(z_s)\dd s\Big)}_{\text{convexity}} . $$

The convexity term scales with the squared implied short-rate vol $\sigma_r\approx\lVert\nabla D_\psi\rVert\,\sigma_z$. A realistic $\sigma_r\sim1\%$/yr gives $\sim 20$ bp of 10-year convexity; an uncontrolled $\sigma_r\sim40\%$/yr gives hundreds of percent, saturating the discount factor and destroying the curve — the central reason volatility must be pinned.

Treasury futures

For delivery $T_f$, basket $\mathcal B$ and conversion factors $\{\mathrm{CF}_i\}$,

$$ F=\E^{\QQ}\!\Big[\min_{i\in\mathcal B}\frac{B_i(z_{T_f})}{\mathrm{CF}_i}\Big], $$

the expected cheapest-to-deliver (CTD) ratio. The forward deliverable-bond prices $B_i$ come from a learned BondNet $B_\chi(z,b_i)$, avoiding a nested simulation.

3. Identifiability problems

Development repeatedly revealed that the calibration data does not constrain quantities the model needs. The three structural ones:

  1. Volatility is not identified. Yields depend on $\sigma$ only through bp-level convexity; futures only weakly, via CTD optionality. Left free, the trained $\sigma_r$ collapsed to $0.1$–$0.4\%$/yr and the Monte-Carlo fan degenerated to a single line.
  2. The futures head was decoupled from the rates. BondNet is a free function of $z$; nothing tied it to the discount factors of the same paths. A naive consistency penalty whose gradient flowed into the SDE bent the yield curve to meet BondNet's par-anchored prices, a spurious $-250$ to $-300$ bp droop in 5–10y yields.
  3. The term premium is not identified under $\QQ$. A pure risk-neutral fit says nothing about physical dynamics; the market price of risk $\lambda$ is unobservable from prices alone.

Two mechanism-level issues were also fixed: an OU drift with unbounded mean-reversion $\kappa$ erases $z_0$ over $1/\kappa$, collapsing the curve to a constant (since $y(\tau)\propto g(\kappa\tau)$, $g(x)=1-(1-e^{-x})/x\to1$); and scale/unit faults (percent vs. decimal, a $365$-vs-$252$ day-count mismatch between the calendar-year maturity grid and date-derived horizons).

4. Resolving terms in the objective

$$ \mathcal L= \lambda_y\mathcal L_{\text{yield}} +\lambda_f\mathcal L_{\text{fut}} +\lambda_c\mathcal L_{\text{cons}} +\lambda_\sigma\mathcal L_{\text{vol}} +\lambda_{\PP}\mathcal L_{\PP/\QQ}. $$

Yield / futures. Absolute yield MSE on decimals; futures use a relative error so the weights are commensurable (absolute price MSE on $\$120$ contracts otherwise dwarfs yields by $10^3$–$10^5$).

BondNet–SDE consistency (LSMC). Discount each deliverable's reconstructed cashflows pathwise on the model's own rates, $\hat B_i^{(n)}=\sum_j c_{ij}\,e^{-(I^{(n)}_{t_{ij}}-I^{(n)}_{T_f})}$, and regress BondNet onto it:

$$ \mathcal L_{\text{cons}} =\frac1{100^2}\,\E_n\!\Big[\big(B_\chi(z^{(n)}_{T_f},b_i)-\sg[\hat B_i^{(n)}]\big)^2\Big]. $$

$\E[\hat B_i\mid z_{T_f}]$ is the model-consistent bond price (Longstaff– Schwartz), so $B_\chi$ learns it with no nested simulation. The stop-gradient $\sg[\cdot]$ is essential: the gradient reaches BondNet only — the non-detached variant is what bent the curve.

Volatility anchor. $\mathcal L_{\text{vol}}=(\operatorname{std}_n[r^{(n)}_{1\mathrm y}]-\sigma^\star)^2$, $\sigma^\star\approx1\%$. Principled because, by Girsanov, the diffusion is measure-invariant, so a $\PP$-estimated vol is a valid prior for the $\QQ$-dynamics.

$\PP/\QQ$ consistency & the term premium. The learned drift is $\mu^{\QQ}$. With market price of risk $\lambda$ and $\dd W^{\QQ}=\dd W^{\PP}+\lambda\,\dd s$, Girsanov gives

$$ \mu^{\PP}(s,z)=\mu^{\QQ}(s,z)+\sigma_\theta(s,z)\,\lambda . $$

A one-step physical forecast is matched to the realised future rate $h$ ahead,

$$ \hat r^{\PP}_{t+h}=x_t+D_\psi\big(z_0+\mu^{\PP}(0,z_0)h\big)-D_\psi(z_0), \qquad \mathcal L_{\PP/\QQ}=\big(\hat r^{\PP}_{t+h}-x_{t+h}\big)^2 . $$

This identifies $\lambda$ — the term premium (forward $=$ expected future rate $+\,\sigma\lambda$). With $\lambda_{\PP}=0$ the model is a pure $\QQ$-calibration ($\lambda\equiv0$); the grid runs both as a primary axis.

The volatility and term-premium terms are anchors/priors, not fits — a property of the data, not of the architecture.

6. Open problems and future work

  1. The single-$z_0$ bottleneck. All day-specific information enters only through the initial condition. Conditioning the drift (and the OU long-run mean $\vartheta_\theta$) on the encoder embedding would make the dynamics day-specific and fix the OU pathology at its root.
  2. One-factor decoder. A linear decoder makes $r=w^\top z+b$ — a one-factor model regardless of $d$. A multi-layer decoder (gridded) restores multi-factor curvature.
  3. Honest model selection. Select on a per-maturity yield RMSE (bp) on a held-out temporal fold, not the aggregate loss whose scale is dominated by anchor terms.
  4. A richer $\PP$ model. The current $\PP/\QQ$ term is a local, one-step drift match with constant $\lambda$. A full treatment makes $\lambda=\lambda(s,z)$ and matches the $\PP$-transition density over the window (a likelihood), giving a time-varying term premium.
  5. Arbitrage-consistent futures. Price deliverables directly off the simulated curve so futures are arbitrage-consistent with yields by construction, rather than via an expectation-matched regression.

Status. The front of the curve fits to $\pm25$ bp, training is stable, futures price to $\sim1\%$. The failure mode under investigation is a long-end drift droop; the detached LSMC term, the volatility anchor, and the $\PP/\QQ$ axis target it directly, with falsifiable signatures: a non-degenerate fan of width $\sim\sigma^\star\sqrt\tau$, a flat per-maturity error, and a recovered $\lambda$ consistent with a positive term premium.
