IRM2: A Neural Short-Rate Model for Joint Calibration of Treasury Yield Curves and Futures

Construction, pricing, diagnostics, and open problems

Project report · June 2026 · companion to report/project_report.tex

Abstract. IRM2 calibrates a latent-state neural stochastic differential equation (SDE) to the U.S. Treasury yield curve and Treasury futures. A recurrent encoder maps a window of observed curves to a latent initial condition; a Neural SDE evolves it under the risk-neutral measure $\QQ$; a decoder produces the short rate; and a Monte-Carlo pricer values zero-coupon bonds and the cheapest-to-deliver futures, the latter through an auxiliary deliverable-bond network (BondNet). We specify the model construction (§1), the pricing maps (§2) and the $\QQ$-objective (§3); catalogue the problems found during development by type — mathematical, architectural, engineering (§4); describe the optional physical-measure extension (§5); and list open questions (§6).

1. Model construction

The model is a composition $\text{history}\xrightarrow{E_\phi} z_0 \xrightarrow{\text{SDE}_\theta} (z_s) \xrightarrow{D_\psi}(r_s)\to\text{prices}$, with an auxiliary network $B_\chi$ for deliverable bonds.

1.1 Encoder

Fix a lookback length $L$ and stride $\nu$. The input at date $t$ is the window of the $M$ SVENY yield pillars stacked with the overnight rate $x$,

$$ X_t=\big[\mathbf c_{t-\nu(L-1)},\dots,\mathbf c_t\,;\,x_{t-\nu(L-1)},\dots,x_t\big]\in\R^{L\times(M+1)}. $$

A fixed preprocessing rescales to percent units, $\pi(X)=100\,X$ (raw decimal yields move by $\sim 5\times10^{-4}$/day — too weak a signal; problem E3). The latent initial state is

$$ z_0=\mathcal N\!\Big(W\,\mathrm{LSTM}_\phi^{\leftrightarrow}\big(\pi(X_t)\big)_{L}+b\Big)\in\R^d, $$

a bidirectional LSTM (last step), an affine readout to $\R^d$, and an output normalisation $\mathcal N\in\{\text{LayerNorm},\text{RMSNorm}\}$ — which fixes $\lVert z_0\rVert$, so the direction of $z_0$ carries the day. A hierarchical variant combines fast/slow streams. $z_0$ is the only day-specific quantity in the model (problem A1).

1.2 Latent dynamics (Neural SDE)

$$ \dd z_s=\mu_\theta(s,z_s)\,\dd s+\sigma_\theta(s,z_s)\,\dd W_s^{\QQ}, \qquad z_s\in\R^d,\ \text{diagonal noise}. $$

Time enters as an input, $(s,z)\mapsto[z\,|\,s]$ (time-inhomogeneous). Two drift families,

$$ \text{(simple)}\ \mu_\theta=f_\theta(s,z),\qquad \text{(OU)}\ \mu_\theta=\kappa_\theta(s,z)\odot(\vartheta_\theta(s,z)-z),\ \ 0\le\kappa_\theta\le\kappa_{\max}, $$

with MLPs $f_\theta,\vartheta_\theta,\kappa_\theta$ (softplus on $\kappa$). The diffusion $\sigma_\theta=\eta\,\zeta_\theta$ factors a magnitude scale $\eta$ (set by construction; problem M2) from a constant or MLP shape $\zeta_\theta\ge0$. Integration is Euler–Maruyama with step $\Delta=1/\texttt{spy}$ over $N$ paths sharing $z_0$.

1.3 Decoder and short-rate anchor

$$ r_s=D_\psi(z_s)+\big(x_t-D_\psi(z_0)\big)\;\Longrightarrow\; r_s=x_t+\big(D_\psi(z_s)-D_\psi(z_0)\big),\quad r_0=x_t. $$

The bracket is path-independent ($z_0$ shared). A linear $D_\psi=w^\top z+b$ makes $r$ a scalar projection $r_s=x_t+w^\top(z_s-z_0)$ — one factor regardless of $d$ (problem A2); a multilayer $D_\psi$ restores curvature.

1.4 BondNet: deliverable bond pricing

Pricing futures requires, at delivery $T_f$, each deliverable bond's forward price as a function of $z_{T_f}$ — itself a conditional expectation $\E^{\QQ}[\cdot\mid z_{T_f}]$. Instead of a nested simulation, a network learns it. Each deliverable $i$ has static features

$$ b_i=(\text{yrs-to-mat},\text{yrs-to-next-cpn},\text{yrs-from-last-cpn}, c_i,f_i,n_i,\text{accr.frac},\text{accr.int})\in\R^8, $$

and the two-branch late-fusion form is

$$ B_\chi(z,b_i)=\rho\big(g_{\text{fus}}[\,g_z(z)\,;\,g_b(b_i)\,]\big), \qquad \rho=\text{softplus}, $$

MLP branches $g_z,g_b$ and a fusion head returning a dirty price per $100$ face; the output bias is initialised near par ($\$100$) so training starts in range (problem E1). $B_\chi$ is a priori an unconstrained function of $z$ — not tied to the simulated discount factors, the defect A3 behind the consistency term.

2. Risk-neutral pricing

2.1 Zero-coupon bonds and yields

With the left-Riemann integral $I^{(n)}_\tau=\Delta\sum_{s_k\le\tau}r^{(n)}_{s_k}$ and grid index $k(\tau)=\mathrm{round}(\tau\cdot\texttt{spy})$,

$$ P(0,\tau)=\E^{\QQ}[e^{-I_\tau}] =\exp\!\big(\operatorname*{logsumexp}_n(-I^{(n)}_\tau)-\log N\big), \qquad y(\tau)=-\frac{\log P(0,\tau)}{\tau}, $$

the log-sum-exp being the stable Monte-Carlo mean (clamped to $P\in[10^{-12},1]$); maturities are the integer pillars $\tau\in\{1,\dots,M\}$.

Convexity. A cumulant expansion of $\log\E^{\QQ}[e^{-J}]$, $J=\int_0^\tau D_\psi(z_s)\dd s$, gives

$$ y(\tau)\approx \underbrace{(x_t-D_\psi(z_0))}_{\text{level}} +\underbrace{\tfrac1\tau\!\int_0^\tau\!\E[D_\psi(z_s)]\dd s}_{\text{expectations}} -\underbrace{\tfrac1{2\tau}\Var\!\big(\!\int_0^\tau D_\psi(z_s)\dd s\big)}_{\text{convexity}}. $$

Convexity scales with $\sigma_r^2$, $\sigma_r\approx\lVert\nabla D_\psi\rVert\sigma_z$: $\sigma_r\sim1\%$/yr gives $\sim20$ bp at $10$y; $\sigma_r\sim40\%$/yr gives hundreds of percent and saturates the clamp (problems M1, M2).

2.2 Treasury futures (cheapest-to-deliver)

Futures are daily-settled, hence undiscounted. Let $\iota_f=\max\{k:s_k\le T_f\}$ be the no-look-ahead delivery index (a searchsorted); for basket $\mathcal B$ with conversion factors $\{\cf_i\}$,

$$ F=\E^{\QQ}\!\Big[\min_{i\in\mathcal B}\frac{B_\chi(z_{\iota_f},b_i)}{\cf_i}\Big] =\frac1N\sum_n\min_{i\in\mathcal B}\frac{B_\chi(z^{(n)}_{\iota_f},b_i)}{\cf_i}. $$

The inner $\min$ is the cheapest-to-deliver option (the short's choice); across the contracts active on a date the per-basket minima are a single segmented reduction (scatter-amin). The short-rate target $\E[r_0]=x_t$ is exact by the anchor and carries no information (problem E4).

3. Risk-neutral training objective

$$ \mathcal L_{\QQ}= \lambda_y\mathcal L_{\text{yield}}+\lambda_f\mathcal L_{\text{fut}} +\lambda_c\mathcal L_{\text{cons}}+\lambda_\sigma\mathcal L_{\text{vol}}. $$
  • Yield — absolute MSE on decimals, $\frac1M\sum_\tau(y(\tau)-y^\star(\tau))^2$.
  • Futures — relative error $\frac1{|\mathcal F|}\sum_k((F_k-F^\star_k)/F^\star_k)^2$ (dimensionless; problem E5).
  • BondNet–SDE consistency (LSMC). Discount each deliverable's reconstructed cashflows pathwise on the model's own rates, $\hat B_i^{(n)}=\sum_j c_{ij}e^{-(I^{(n)}_{t_{ij}}-I^{(n)}_{T_f})}$, and $$ \mathcal L_{\text{cons}}=\tfrac1{100^2}\E_n\big[(B_\chi(z^{(n)}_{T_f},b_i)-\sg[\hat B_i^{(n)}])^2\big], $$ a Longstaff–Schwartz regression with stop-gradient $\sg[\cdot]$ (gradient to BondNet only; problem A3).
  • Volatility anchor $\mathcal L_{\text{vol}}=(\operatorname{std}_n[r^{(n)}_{1\mathrm y}]-\sigma^\star)^2$, $\sigma^\star\approx1\%$ (problem M1).

4. Problems encountered

The development history is a sequence of identifiability and conditioning failures, grouped by type.

4.1 Mathematical

  1. M1. Volatility is not identified. Yields depend on $\sigma$ only through bp-level convexity; futures only weakly via CTD optionality. Trained $\sigma_r$ collapsed to $0.1$–$0.4\%$/yr and the fan degenerated to a line. Fix: the anchor $\mathcal L_{\text{vol}}$ (legitimate — $\sigma$ is measure-invariant by Girsanov).
  2. M2. Convexity blow-up. A diffusion left to the softplus floor gave $\sigma_r\sim40\%$/yr; convexity then swamps every yield. Fix: the scale $\eta$ sets $\sigma_r\approx1\%$ by construction.
  3. M3. OU erases the encoder signal. $y(\tau)\approx(x_t-D_0)+A\,g(\kappa\tau)$, $g(x)=1-(1-e^{-x})/x\to1$ as $\kappa\to\infty$: large $\kappa$ flattens the curve to a constant independent of $z_0$. Fix: cap $\kappa\le\kappa_{\max}$.

4.2 Architectural

  1. A1. Single-$z_0$ bottleneck. All day-specific information enters through the initial condition; drift, diffusion, decoder are global. The model only re-aims a fixed system per day — limiting expressiveness and enabling M3.
  2. A2. One-factor decoder. A linear $D_\psi$ makes $r$ a scalar projection of $z$; the curve is one-factor regardless of $d$. Mitigation: multilayer decoder (a grid axis).
  3. A3. Decoupled futures head. BondNet is a free function of $z$, not tied to the simulated discount factors, so the model is not internally arbitrage-consistent. Mitigation: the LSMC term. A non-detached version let its (dominant) gradient flow into the SDE and bent the curve toward BondNet's par prices ($-250$ to $-300$ bp long-end droop) — hence the stop-gradient.

4.3 Engineering / training

  1. E1. Gradient explosion / NaN. Back-prop through a $\sim10^3$-step Euler unroll multiplies one Jacobian per step. Fix: $\Delta=1/32$, near-identity init, coefficient scales, gradient clipping, a non-finite-gradient guard, near-par BondNet init.
  2. E2. Stochastic SDE coefficients. Dropout in the drift/ diffusion MLPs draws a fresh mask per Euler step — the trained process differs from the deterministic evaluated one. Fix: dropout removed from all SDE networks.
  3. E3. Encoder input scale. Raw decimal yields move by $\sim5\times10^{-4}$/day. Fix: percent-unit preprocessing.
  4. E4. Dead short-rate target. $\E[r_0]=x_t$ by the anchor, MSE $\approx0$. Fix: weight $0$.
  5. E5. Loss-scale imbalance. Absolute futures MSE on $\$120$ prices exceeds yield MSE by $10^3$–$10^5$. Fix: relative futures error.
  6. E6. Selection on the wrong metric. The aggregate loss is dominated by anchor / variance-floor terms. Recommendation: per-maturity yield RMSE (bp) on a held-out fold.
  7. E7. Data and unit faults. Percent-vs-decimal rates; a $365$-vs-$252$ day-count inconsistency between the calendar-year maturity grid and date-derived futures horizons (mis-timed deliverable state); a single $\cf=$ NaN poisoning the CTD $\min$. Fix: $\div100$ at load, one business-day/$252$ convention, basket sanitisation.

5. The physical-measure ($\PP/\QQ$) extension

Logically separate: an optional extension, off by default, not part of the core $\QQ$-calibration.

The drift above is the risk-neutral $\mu^{\QQ}$. With market price of risk $\lambda$ and $\dd W^{\QQ}=\dd W^{\PP}+\lambda\,\dd s$, Girsanov gives the physical drift $\mu^{\PP}=\mu^{\QQ}+\sigma_\theta\lambda$ (diffusion measure-invariant). A one-step physical forecast is matched to the realised future rate $h$ ahead,

$$ \hat r^{\PP}_{t+h}=x_t+D_\psi\big(z_0+\mu^{\PP}(0,z_0)h\big)-D_\psi(z_0), \qquad \mathcal L_{\PP/\QQ}=(\hat r^{\PP}_{t+h}-x_{t+h})^2, $$

added as $\lambda_{\PP}\mathcal L_{\PP/\QQ}$. This identifies $\lambda$ — the term premium (forward $=$ expected future rate $+\,\sigma\lambda$). With $\lambda_{\PP}=0$ the model is pure $\QQ$ ($\lambda\equiv0$); the grid runs both as an axis. The implementation is deliberately minimal (constant $\lambda$, local one-step match); a fuller version is in §6.

6. Identifiability summary and open problems

QuantityIdentified by the data?Mechanism
curve level / slopeyes (yields)$\mathcal L_{\text{yield}}$
short-rate volatilityno (vol-insensitive)$\mathcal L_{\text{vol}}$ anchor (M1)
deliverable bond pricesonly via CTD min$\mathcal L_{\text{cons}}$ (A3)

  1. Day-conditioned dynamics. Feed the encoder embedding into $\mu_\theta$ (and the OU $\vartheta_\theta$), not just the initial condition — attacks A1 and M3 at the root.
  2. Multi-factor decoder. Resolve A2 empirically via the decoder grid axis.
  3. Honest selection. Per-maturity bp RMSE on a validation fold (E6).
  4. Richer $\PP$ model. State-dependent $\lambda(s,z)$ and a $\PP$-transition-density likelihood over the window, giving a time-varying term premium.
  5. Arbitrage-consistent futures. Price deliverables directly off the simulated curve, so futures are consistent with yields by construction.

Status. The front of the curve fits to $\pm25$ bp, training is stable, futures price to $\sim1\%$. The failure mode under investigation is a long-end drift droop; the detached LSMC term, the volatility anchor, and day-conditioning target it, with falsifiable signatures: a non-degenerate fan of width $\sim\sigma^\star\sqrt\tau$, a flat per-maturity error, and a recovered $\lambda$ consistent with a positive term premium.
